I put a lot of thought into the links that land in Accessibility Weekly.

How that happens evolves constantly. I keep an up-to-date set of guidance over on a page called [What makes a good link](https://a11yweekly.com/good-links/). Recently, I shared a virtual stage with Jack McElaney ([Accessibility in the News](https://www.microassist.com/digital-access/news/)), and Ricky Onsman ([Weekly Reading List](https://reading-lists.kc.vispero.com)), two other newsletter curators, and it made me realize I should outline more of how I think about what goes into the newsletter in a blog post. Much of what I’ll cover here gets a mention on that virtual stage though. [OZeWAI](https://ozewai.org/), a professional accessibility organization based in Australia, ran the event. You can watch the stream, called [Accessibility News - Ask the Professionals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X0ie8WmDp5k) on YouTube for all the accessibility nerd content. Huge thanks to Amanda Mace for moderating with thoughtful questions, and Jack and Ricky for participating.

In the meantime, let’s dive into how I decide on links that end up in Accessibility Weekly.

As mentioned, I use that good links page as a guide. I’m not going to rehash that page here, but will mention the most important aspects.

## Help People Learn Digital Accessibility

I started the newsletter to help people learn and make accessibility less intimidating. This trumps all other requirements when it comes to the posts and resources I share. It should help someone interested in digital accessibility bring new knowledge to their everyday work.

## Present Current, Accurate Information with a Perspective

I want a link that provides current, accurate information while bringing a unique perspective. Readers should have confidence in the information available. I remember reading forum posts when I first started learning accessibility. Many contained levels of nuance I wasn’t ready to understand. That’s part of accessibility, but I want the links I select for Accessibility Weekly to have a level of clarity that those forum posts lacked. That clarity can show up in different ways, but the reader should be able to walk away with an action or a concept to investigate further, thanks to a new understanding.

## Feature the Lived Experience of People with Disabilities

I try to bring the perspectives of people with disabilities to readers of the newsletter. I want to feature posts that talk about everyday life, not only content centered on using technology. As much as I aim to do this, other sources do it better. [Disability Debrief](https://www.disabilitydebrief.org) stands out as one.

## Include Content Written by a Human on an Independent Domain

Social media sites come and go. I try to feature posts on domains owned by independent companies or people because they have a chance to survive longer. I want content that someone invested time and resources into before putting it out there. That means there's a higher chance a human crafted it.

## Bring More Insight Than a Tool Alone

We need tools that help accessibility specialists do their job. I do include them now and then. What excites me more? How did you make the tool? What decisions did you make and why? Those are the stories I want to share. Tools alone don’t often convey that.

## Stick to a Boring Structure

The newsletter hasn’t changed much over the years. It has three main sections and compiles about 10 to 15 links each week. Here’s how I approach each section:

**Featured post**: I want this to bring depth and make people think. It could have an unpopular opinion, be a long tutorial or a less broached topic. I hope people read it, and think about it throughout the week.

**News, resources, tools and tutorials**: This functions like a grab bag. I hope everyone finds something in here to click on and explore. I try to include accessibility-adjacent links in addition to the regular topic. It might be anything from performance to HTML to design systems.

**New to A11y**: I reserve this section for beginner-level posts. I want this content to bring people along slowly. I don’t mind covering the same topics, especially when written by different authors or from different perspectives.

Chatting about creating and curating accessibility news and links was a huge blast. Here’s to more links coming people’s way.
